A harvest is not a to-do list.

It is ten scattered fields, six weeks of weather, whoever turned up to pick, and a number at the end that you will want again next year. A notebook loses it. A group chat buries it. Ventema was built around the way the work actually happens.

Built for standing in a field

Big rows, plain numbers, one tap to log. Everything works with cold hands and no signal — the app needs no connection to do its job.

The whole family, one farm

It runs on your own iCloud. Every device signed into the account sees the same groves, the same plan, the same totals — no invites, no accounts, no sharing links.

Every season kept

This winter's picking never overwrites last winter's. Switch season and the whole app moves with it, so you can put two years side by side.

How a season goes

From the first pin in October to the last branch burned in March.

1

Put the farm on the map

Walk into a grove and drop a pin where you stand, or place it by hand on the map. Give it a name, the number of trees, and how badly it wants picking.

After that the map does the thinking: the ones still to do, the ones in progress, the one that always ripens first — all in front of you at once, in the colours the app uses everywhere.

Let it plan the picking

Ventema lays the remaining groves across the days you intend to work, urgent ones first, and orders each day so you are not driving back past a grove you have already passed.

Then the weather turns and you move things anyway — drag a grove to another day and the plan closes up behind it.

Log what it gave

Back from the mill, put the litres in. Kilos of olives, working days — half days count as half — and how many pickers stood in the field. The grove moves to picked and the season's totals add themselves up.

That is the part you will want next October, when the question is whether the low grove is worth the four days it costs.

Finish the job properly

The picking ends and the rest begins: pruning, branches to burn, ground to clear. Each job sits with the grove it belongs to, takes a date, and turns red on its own once that date has gone by.

By March the list is empty, the season is closed, and next year's is waiting behind it.

Everything in it

Four tabs. The whole of a harvest.

Every grove on a map

Drop a pin while standing in the grove, or place it by hand. Each pin carries its trees, its priority and where it stands in the season.

A plan for the winter

Which grove on which day, built from what is left and how urgent it is — and rearranged the moment the weather says otherwise.

What each grove gave

Litres of oil, kilos of olives, working days and pickers. The season totals add themselves up as you go.

The tidying up afterwards

Pruning, branches to burn, ground to clear — hung off the grove they belong to, with dates that go red when they pass.

The shortest way round

A day with four groves in it has a best order to drive them. Ventema works it out, and hands any grove to Apple Maps for directions.

The family in step

What your father logs in the grove is on your phone by the time you get back to the house — through your own iCloud, with no account.

English and Greek

The whole app, in both — Καλή σοδειά. Dates and litres come out in your own region's format.

Seasons that stay apart

A season is named for the autumn it opens in. Switch it in Settings and the map, the plan and the list all move with it.

Your farm stays yours

No account. No servers. No analytics.

Ventema has no back end. Your groves live on your device and, if you leave iCloud on, in your own private iCloud database — the one nobody else can read, the developer included. There is nothing to sign up for, no tracking, and no third-party software inside the app collecting anything about you or your farm.
